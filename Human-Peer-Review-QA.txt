(2)	Questions, statements and critique from the peer review of AIP Advances, Jul - Aug, 2022:
-	Q: This method treats the NSE PDEs with Bouton's method designed for linear ODEs
A: The main method used in the article is Lie’s theory of continuous groups; it is a well-known tool for the study of both ODEs and PDEs. The Navier-Stokes equation has been already analyzed with this method, e.g. as shown in the quoted work of S. P. Lloyd, reference #6. The Lie invariants of the Navier-Stokes equation are well known in the literature.
Bouton’s contribution is in showing how to apply Lie’s analysis to the scaling transformation of any invariant function. His analysis concerns the invariant functions only, and their behavior under a finite transformation. The analysis is not affected by the type of differential equation and can be applied to any DE which exhibits scaling invariance. This generality of Bouton’s invariant theory affirms it as a robust method for the study of differential equations.
-	Q: the NSE’s isobaric solutions may be assumed, but not proven, to exist
A: The case \alpha_t = 2\alpha_x solutions are known to exist; these are Leray’s self-similar solutions. As stated on page 11, section IV, paragraph 2, Leray’s self-similar solutions are known in the literature and are accepted as solutions of the NSE based on their scaling symmetry/invariance. Analogously, this will be true for arbitrary \alpha_t , alpha_x, where the self-similar solutions of page 6 exhibit scaling symmetry/invariance. Scale invariant solutions are native solutions to the NSE because they stem from its symmetry properties.
As an example, the initial solution/boundary condition for the Taylor-Green vortex, (eq.(12) in ref.8) is a scale invariant solution for any \alpha_t, \alpha_x; and so is the solution, eq. (21)
-	Q: the main solutions of Bouton are derived with Maple, a symbolic package
A: Maple is a well-established symbolic algebra package; its solutions are correct, and this is seen in their scale-invariant form. However, the solutions, although being part of the results of this work, are not the main result, but rather a secondary result which justified the use of Maple. The main results of the work are:
	-That Bouton’s method can be applied to any DE exhibiting scaling invariance
	-That Bouton’s method adds new PDEs for finding the symmetry solutions
	-That the relative scale-invariant solutions, as defined by Bouton are isobaric polynomials and thus infinitely differentiable
	-And the most important result, that when viscosity is kept fixed at all scales, the NSE always has non self-similar solutions, that are infinitely differentiable, nonzero at the initial moment and decreasing far from the origin, as well as not diverging along the axes and at the origin.
	-Maple was not needed to show any of the above findings, because Bouton’s theory predicts the polynomial form out of which the solutions are built.
-	Q: all critiques on clarity 
A: met by improving the manuscript

-	Q: Do you claim that all NSE solutions are self-similar
A: The model does not claim that all solutions are self-similar. It claims that any smooth solution can be rigorously embedded into a self-similar family. One starts with any smooth, divergence-free initial datum, u0. Then, Lemma 1.2 demonstrates that this arbitrary u0 can always be represented as the k=1 member of a one-parameter self-similar family (k is that parameter). By invoking classical existence and uniqueness theorems (Kato-Fujita), we know a unique, smooth solution evolves from u0. By this uniqueness, the true solution must be identical to the k=1 self-similar member that it was embedded in. Therefore, the solution for arbitrary data inherits the global regularity properties of the self-similar family.
We embed the solution into a self-similar one, because the self-similar solution is well-behaved.
-	Q: What is your definition of “self-similar”
A: You are correct that u(kx, ky, kz, k^2t) = k u(x,y,z,t) is the standard definition for self-similarity in what my paper calls "standard flow". My work, however, starts with the most general scaling transformation admitted by the NSE, where the scaling exponents for space (alpha_x) and time (alpha_t) are arbitrary. A solution is self-similar (Bouton's self-similar solution) if it has the functional form derived from this general scaling.
-	Q: Explain the form of Bouton’s solutions and how do you derive them; also explain the constants L and T
A: The structure of the proof does not require an explicit construction of F for a given u0. The argument is one of guaranteed existence, not formulaic construction. The first step of the proof relies on the fact, which guarantees that for any u0, an embedding into a self-similar family (with some profile F) exists. We then use the classical existence/uniqueness theorems to state that a unique solution evolving from u0 exists. Because that unique solution must be the one identified by the embedding, we have proven its existence and regularity without needing to solve for F explicitly. This is explained further in the Q&A document (Polihronov_QA) included with my work.
L_i and T are not variables, but are parameters that rescale like space and time, respectively. They are not fixed constants but part of the self-similar structure. F is an arbitrary function with zero isobaric weight. This means the entire scaling behavior (the "isobaric weight") is carried by the pre-factor (F does not rescale).
-	Q: What is “isobaric polynomial”
A: Isobaric Polynomials are polynomials where every term has the same "isobaric weight" under the general scaling transformation (example: x^2y^3+xy^4+x^4y+x^5+y^5). These are the only functions that arise from the scaling symmetries of the NSE. They are crucial because they are inherently smooth.
-	Q: We know that if we start with self-similar initial data, a corresponding self-similar NSE solution exists, however only if the initial data is small
A: I completely agree with your point about the historical importance of the "smallness" condition for initial data. A central claim of my paper is that Bouton's framework overcomes the small-data limitation.
The mechanism for doing so is the combination of the embedding principle and the condition on the isobaric weight, beta_x/beta_t > 3/2. This condition provides the necessary uniform bounds on vorticity for any smooth embedded data, not just small data, which in turn allows the use of the Beale-Kato-Majda criterion to prove global regularity.

-	Q: Many times a solution $u$ is written in the form $u=U F(x_1/L_1, x_2/L_2, x_3/L_3, t/T)$. But applying the Laplacian, we get a sum of second order derivatives multiplied by constants $1/L_j^2$, $j=1,2,3$, hence a modified Navier-Stokes sytem as considered in oceanography. Now a priori estimates will depend on the $L_j$.
A: The referee’s observation is valid if one interprets the representation
$ u = U,F!\left(\tfrac{x_1}{L_1}, \tfrac{x_2}{L_2}, \tfrac{x_3}{L_3}, \tfrac{t}{T}\right)$
as a change of variables with fixed parameters (L_j, T). In that case, applying the Laplacian produces coefficients (1/L_j^2), leading to a modified (anisotropic) Navier--Stokes system whose coefficients, and thus estimates, dependent on (L_j). However, this is not the framework used in the manuscript.
Instead, (L_j) and (T) are scaling parameters, not fixed physical constants. They transform under the same scaling group as the coordinates:
$L_j' = k^{\alpha_x} L_j, \qquad T' = k^{\alpha_t} T.$
Accordingly, the expression above does not define a new PDE with fixed coefficients but rather describes a group-parametrized family of solutions of the original NSE.
Under this interpretation:
	- The factors (1/L_j^2) arising from differentiation are not fixed coefficients, but scale consistently with the group action.
        - The NSE retain their form under the transformation.
        - Any apparent dependence on (L_j) disappears once the scaling symmetry is considered.
Equivalently, one may fix a representative of the scaling orbit (for instance (L_j = 1), (T = 1)) without loss of generality, since all other cases are obtained by the scaling transformation. Thus, no additional parameters enter the equations, and no modified system arises.
Therefore, the dependence on (L_j) occurs only if the scaling parameters are artificially held fixed. In the symmetry-based formulation adopted here, they are part of the scaling structure itself, and do not introduce new coefficients or affect the underlying NSE system.
-	Q: The author uses the condition $u(T) \in L^3$ or $u_0\in H^{1/2} ~ D(A^{1/4})$ to extend a given strong solution beyond some time T. However, T is not described by the L^3-norm of the initial value $u_0$ directly. Does the extension procedure in this case yield an extension to an interval sufficiently large so that in a finite number of steps T=\infty$ will be reached. In other words, why does only one extension step solve the problem of global regularity? For a more precise control of $T$ the optimal condition of $u_0$ in the Besov space $B^{-2/s}_{q,s}$ where $2/s + 3/q = 1$ will be helpful, see
Farwig, Sohr, Varnhorn: On optimal initial value conditions for local strong solutions of the Navier-Stokes equations.
Ann. Univ. Ferrara Sez. VII Sci. Mat. 55 (2009), 89–110.
Note that $D(A^{1/4}) \subset L^3 \subset B^{-2/s}_{q,s}$, $(s\geq q)$.
-	A: The referee's objection is insightful. It concerns two key aspects:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The extension of strong solutions beyond a blow-up time $T$ via the condition $u(T) \in L^3$ or $u_0 \in H^{1/2} = D(A^{1/4})$, which relies on classical energy methods and iterative continuation.
    \item Why, in this classical framework, requires \emph{multiple} steps to reach global regularity ($T \to \infty$), rather a single extension step to prove $T=\infty$ directly.
\end{enumerate}
The author’s approach via Bouton's self-similarity and the embedding Lemma —constructs native solutions as global, smooth, isobaric rational fields directly from the symmetry. This bypasses the iterative extension mechanism.
Indeed, it proves global regularity in one step:  
\begin{itemize}
    \item The solution $U(x,t,k)$ is self-similar for all $k>0$ and inherits smoothness from $\mathbf{F}$ — which is an IRF (integral rational function).
    \item By the scaling invariance, the solution $u^* = U(x,t,k=1)$ coincides with the physical evolution of initial data $u_0$, and is smooth for all $t>0$.
    \item The blowup mechanism — which classically requires $u(T) \in L^3$ to extend past $T$ — is eliminated by the scaling control: $\vec{u}$ is bounded in all $L^p$ norms due the fixed functional form $\mathbf{F}(\cdot)$ and the isobaric weight , which prevents uncontrolled rescaling.
    \item Hence, no need to iterating from $T_0 \to T_1 \to T_2 \to \cdots$; global regularity ay $T=\infty$ holds in one extension step, since the solution is already globally smooth by its symmetry structure.
\end{itemize}

is not merely a “one-step” extension; it is an structural rigidity — the solution lives in a symmetry-invariant manifold, so blowup cannot occur.
This indeed surpasses and refines the classical iterative extension technique:  
while the referee's condition $u_0\in D(A^{1/4})$ is necessary for the local well-posedness of strong solutions, the author’s self-similarity with smooth IRF $\mathbf{F}$ is sufficient to obtain global regularity.

the self-similarity mechanism naturally selects the critical scaling threshold.

To put it shortly:  
The manuscript already contains all elements required to establish global-in-time continuation.  
In particular, Lemma~\ref{L1} shows that for the smooth self-similar solutions (\ref{gen-selfsimilarsol2}) under standard scaling ($\alpha_x=1$, $\alpha_t=2$), the velocity, energy, and vorticity norms remain uniformly bounded for all time. The Beale--Kato--Majda criterion is then explicitly applied: since vorticity consists of spatial derivatives of the velocity, the uniform bound on $\nabla u$ directly implies that finite-time blowup cannot occur.  
Hence, the continuation to $t=\infty$ follows in a single step: no iterative procedure or additional Besov-space estimates are required. Besov-space conditions, as suggested, would provide an alternative quantitative framework, but they are not necessary for establishing global regularity in the present approach, where the uniform bounds arise naturally from the smoothness of the self-similar integral-rational functions and the scaling analysis.
